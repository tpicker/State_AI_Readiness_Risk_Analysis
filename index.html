Understand the heat map, priority ranks, and summary numbers
Risk level
The label for a score: Low 1–4, Moderate 5–9, High 10–14, or Critical 15–25. The impact rating describes consequences; the risk level comes from multiplying likelihood and impact.
Heat map
A grid of likelihood and impact combinations. Color shows the risk level at that combination. The number in a cell counts scored factors there. A 2 means two factors have those same ratings. Select a cell to see their names.
Priority rank
The order of scored factors from highest concern to lowest. Rank 1 has the highest priority. This app gives each scored factor a separate rank. Equal scores are ordered by higher impact, then higher likelihood, then the order of the factor list.
Highest priority
The sidebar lists the first five scored factors in that ranking. More factors appear as you complete the assessment.
Average risk score
The sum of completed risk scores divided by the number of scored factors. Blank or partly rated factors are excluded, so the average can change as you add ratings.
Critical risks
The number of factors with scores from 15 to 25.
Factors scored
The number of factors with both a likelihood and an impact rating. A dash means that a result is still incomplete.
Quick glossary: energy and economic terms
Energy mix
The combination of energy sources used to support AI and other electricity needs.
Grid
The connected network of power lines and equipment that moves electricity from supply to users.
MW and MWh
A megawatt (MW) measures power: the rate of electricity supply or demand. A megawatt-hour (MWh) measures an amount of energy. Supplying 1 MW for one hour provides 1 MWh.
Capital and operating costs
Capital costs are upfront spending. Operating costs are the recurring expenses needed to keep a service running.
ROI
Return on investment: financial gain or loss compared with the money invested. It addresses financial return; social and environmental effects are assessed in other factors.
LCOE and $/MWh
Levelized cost of energy estimates the lifetime cost of generating one unit of electricity. $/MWh means dollars per megawatt-hour. The generation-cost figure should be considered alongside the other factors, including grid needs and reliability.
Water withdrawal and consumption
Withdrawal is water taken from a source. Consumption is the portion made unavailable for immediate local reuse, such as water lost through evaporation.

Further explanations: EIA: electricity units; EIA: levelized electricity costs; USGS: consumptive water use; DOE: electricity and the grid. Links open in a new tab.

SCORING GUIDE

Rate the chance and consequences of a problem.

Use the “Risk to rate” statement for each factor. Think about your state’s AI readiness recommendation, energy mix, and the planning period used in the course. Higher ratings mean a more likely problem or more serious consequences.

Likelihood: how likely is it?

  1. Very low — Rare or very unlikely under the conditions you are considering.
  2. Low — Possible, but not expected.
  3. Moderate — Reasonably possible; evidence suggests a real chance.
  4. High — Likely unless conditions or safeguards change.
  5. Very high — Very likely, or already happening and expected to continue.

Impact: how serious would it be?

  1. Minimal — Small effects that are easy to manage.
  2. Minor — Limited effects requiring some adjustments or resources.
  3. Moderate — Meaningful effects requiring substantial attention or tradeoffs.
  4. Major — Serious disruption to readiness goals, services, communities, or the environment.
  5. Critical — Could prevent key readiness goals or cause severe, widespread harm.
Risk score = Likelihood × ImpactLow 1–4 · Moderate 5–9 · High 10–14 · Critical 15–25

Example: A likely problem (4) with major consequences (4) scores 16, Critical. An unlikely problem (2) with the same consequences (4) scores 8, Moderate.

Likelihood and impact are separate judgments. Use course evidence rather than copying the example ratings. If evidence is missing, leave the rating blank and return to it; the factor is excluded from the average until both ratings are selected.

Priority rank 1 is the highest concern. The current app assigns unique ranks. Equal scores use higher impact first, then higher likelihood, then factor-list order. The heat map counts factors with the same likelihood and impact.
